CareCue Privacy Policy

Last updated / Effective date: August 16, 2026

1. Introduction

CareCue is a private, opt-in companion app that helps parents and caregivers stay present during baby-care moments by letting them start a “Care Shift,” choose which of their own apps to keep an eye on, and get a gentle nudge if they’ve been on one of those apps for a while. CareCue is not a monitoring or surveillance tool, and it does not watch, score, or report on anyone but the parent using it.

The ten-second version

  • What CareCue collects: what you type or choose in the app (your Care Shifts, check-ins, and settings), plus a screen-time summary for the apps you personally choose to track. All of that stays on your phone.
  • What ever leaves your device: a crash report if the app breaks, and two small, routine technical background requests (explained in full in Section 5) — nothing about your Care Shifts, check-ins, or settings is ever part of any of them.
  • What you can always do: turn off any permission, delete everything CareCue has stored, or uninstall the app — at any time, without asking anyone’s permission but your own.

Everything below is the full detail, for anyone who wants it — you don’t need to read further than this summary to understand the shape of CareCue’s privacy practices.

2. What CareCue does and does not do

CareCue is a tool for a parent to use on their own phone, about their own phone habits, during time they’ve deliberately set aside for a baby. It is not:

  • A monitoring or parental-control tool aimed at a child, a partner, or anyone other than the person using the app.
  • A tool that scores, grades, or judges how someone is parenting.
  • A tool that reports what you do to anyone else — no partner, family member, employer, caregiver, or other third party is ever notified of your Care Shifts, check-ins, or settings by CareCue.
  • A tool that collects data about a child directly. CareCue has no field, screen, or feature anywhere in the app for a child’s name, birth date, photo, or profile.

Everything CareCue does starts because the parent using it chose to start it — starting a Care Shift, picking which apps to track, and ending a Care Shift are all actions only the person holding the phone takes.

3. Data we collect, and why

This section covers what CareCue reads or stores on your device. See Section 5 for the separate, much shorter list of things that leave your device.

Care Shift, check-in, and settings data

When you start a Care Shift, check in, or change a setting, CareCue records that locally: your Care Shift’s start and end time, your reminder threshold, the apps you’ve chosen to track, how long you’ve used those apps during the shift, your check-in timestamps, and your app settings (like quiet hours). This is the core of what makes the app work, and it is stored only on your device.

Screen-time information for the apps you choose to track

During an active Care Shift, CareCue periodically checks how long you’ve used the apps you selected, using Android’s built-in Usage Access feature. Because of how Android’s Usage Access system works, this check briefly reads foreground-usage totals for every app on your phone, not only the ones you selected — that system-wide information is only ever held in your phone’s memory for a moment, to do the calculation. The only thing ever saved is a single running total for the app(s) you actually chose to track — never a breakdown by app, never timestamps of individual sessions, and never anything about an app you didn’t select.

The “Add an app” picker

When you tap “Add an app” to choose what CareCue should track during a Care Shift, CareCue only ever saves the app(s) you actually tap to select. To show you that picker in the first place, CareCue briefly reads the list of apps installed on your phone, along with each app’s recent (last 30 days) usage total, so it can present you with a real, ranked list to choose from. That list is held in memory only, for as long as the picker is open — it is never saved or sent anywhere, and disappears the moment you close the picker. Only the specific app or apps you select ever get written to your settings.

Notification permission

If you grant notification permission, CareCue uses it for exactly one purpose: delivering the gentle Care Shift reminder (“Just checking in”) when your chosen threshold is reached. CareCue does not send any other kind of notification — no marketing, no re-engagement nudges, nothing else.

The permissions CareCue requests

If you look at CareCue’s permissions on your device or in its Play Store listing, you may see more than the ones described above. That’s because Android bundles in a handful of standard technical permissions from the tools CareCue is built with (like its crash-reporting and reminder- scheduling tools), separate from the ones CareCue itself asks you to grant. 4. Data we do not collect

To be explicit about what CareCue never touches, reads, or stores, in any form:

  • No accounts. There is no sign-up, sign-in, username, or password anywhere in CareCue.
  • No cloud sync or backup of your Care Shift data. Your Care Shifts, check-ins, and settings are never uploaded anywhere by CareCue.
  • No analytics or behavioral tracking. CareCue does not use any analytics, advertising, or telemetry tool of any kind — the only things that ever leave your device are described in full in Section 5, and none of them involve tracking your behavior in the app.
  • No child data. CareCue has no field for a child’s name, birth date, photo, or any other identifying information, and never asks for one.
  • No location data. CareCue never requests or reads your location.
  • No message, browsing, or app-content data. CareCue only ever reads app names and usage durations, as described in Section 3 — never the content of anything you type, read, watch, or browse in another app.
  • No advertising identifiers. CareCue does not use ads or any ad-related identifier.

5. Third-party services

Here is the complete list of everything that ever leaves this device, and why. Nothing else does.

Firebase Crashlytics (by Google) — the one that matters most

If CareCue crashes or encounters an unexpected error (on Android only), a standard crash report is automatically sent to Google’s crash-reporting tool, Firebase Crashlytics, to help us find and fix the problem. That report is a category of technical diagnostic information about your device and the failure — for example, your device model, Android version, the app’s version number, the error message, and the technical trace of what the app was doing at the moment it failed. It never includes your Care Shift, check-in, or settings data. Google’s crash-reporting tool may also generate its own internal technical identifiers as part of how it operates (for example, to group crash reports from the same installation) — this is standard behavior of the tool itself, not something CareCue’s own code requests, sets, or has visibility into.

As part of enabling this crash-reporting tool, the underlying Firebase software also checks in with Google’s servers every time you open the app — not only when something crashes. This is routine technical setup required for the crash-reporting tool to be ready if it’s ever needed; it does not carry any Care Shift, check-in, or settings data, and it is a separate thing from an actual crash report being sent.

There is currently no in-app switch to turn crash reporting off.

Google Fonts — a design request, not tracking

CareCue also downloads a small font file from Google’s servers the first time you open the app (and occasionally again afterward, if that file ever needs to be re-downloaded), so the app’s text displays correctly. This is a standard, automatic request that many apps make — it doesn’t send anything about you, your Care Shifts, or your check-ins. It carries only what any ordinary web request carries, like your phone’s general internet connection information, on its way to fetching a font file.

Google Play Billing — purchase handling, if you choose CareCue Plus

If you choose to purchase CareCue Plus, that purchase is handled entirely by Google Play’s own billing system. CareCue never operates its own payment server and never sees or stores your payment details — it only keeps a record, on your device, of which plan you’re on, so the app knows what you’re entitled to. If you request a restore of a previous purchase, that request also goes through Google Play directly.

6. How your data is stored

Your Care Shift, check-in, and settings data lives in a local database on your device only. CareCue has no cloud storage, no account system, and no CareCue-operated server that any of this data is sent to or backed up on. If you never install another copy of the app, that data exists in exactly one place: your phone.

7. Data retention and deletion

Delete all data, available any time from Settings, permanently erases your Care Shift history, check-ins, and usage totals, and resets your settings back to their defaults. The one thing it deliberately keeps is a record of which CareCue Plus plan you’re on (if any), so deleting your data doesn’t accidentally cost you a purchase you’ve already made. This action cannot be undone.

Uninstalling CareCue removes the app and its local data from your device, the same way uninstalling any Android app does. This is standard operating-system behavior, not a feature CareCue builds or controls.

8. Your choices and rights

Because CareCue doesn’t have accounts or a company-side database of your information, your choices work a little differently than they might with a typical app — everything genuinely available to you lives on your own device:

  • Revoke a permission at any time, through Settings on your phone (for Usage Access) or through CareCue’s own Settings screen (for notifications). Revoking a permission may mean some features, like reminders, stop working until it’s granted again.
  • Delete all your data from within the app at any time (see Section 7).
  • Uninstall the app at any time, which removes CareCue and its local data from your device entirely.

CareCue does not currently offer a way to export a copy of your data, and there is no separate opt-out for crash reporting or an account you could ask us to delete, because none of those mechanisms exist in the app today. If that changes, this policy will be updated to reflect it.

9. Children’s privacy

CareCue is directed at parents and caregivers, about their own phone habits — it is not directed at children, and is not designed as a tool that watches, tracks, or identifies a child in any way. There is no field anywhere in the app for a child’s name, birth date, photo, or any other identifying detail, and CareCue does not knowingly collect personal information directly from anyone under 13.

This reasoning is based on CareCue’s current design, audience, and the absence of any child-directed content or child-identifying data field in the app — it is not a final legal determination. This section will be confirmed by legal review before this policy is published.

10. How we protect your data

Your CareCue data is protected by your device’s own operating-system-level app sandboxing — the same general protection that keeps one app’s files separate from another’s on Android. CareCue does not apply any additional encryption to its local database file beyond that today.
